A curated dining experience combines a restaurant setting with a clearly described format: who it suits, how long it lasts, what is included, and how the meal is intended to feel. That structure can make planning easier, particularly for a group or an occasion.

ExperienceHub is currently a demonstration catalogue. Its booking pages explain concepts but do not take reservations or payments. The steps below are a general guide for evaluating any future bookable experience.

## Follow the information path

Begin with the country, then review the restaurant, experience, and package in order. Each level answers a different question:

1. **Country:** Is the destination relevant to your plans?
2. **Restaurant:** Does the setting and city suit the occasion?
3. **Experience:** Is the dining format what you are looking for?
4. **Package:** Do the guest count, duration, and inclusions fit?

Following this path provides context that a price alone cannot.

## Read the package as a complete offer

Compare the short summary with the detailed inclusions. Look for guest guidance, duration, food and drink format, who the package suits, and important notes.

If a price is labelled “placeholder,” it is not a confirmed business price. Do not use it to set a final budget until the restaurant or booking service publishes an approved amount.

Related packages can help you understand the trade-offs. One may offer a family-friendly shared table, while another focuses on two guests or a social group of friends.

## Prepare the details a venue may need

Before starting a real booking, gather:

- The confirmed number of guests
- Preferred date and a backup option
- Dietary requirements and allergies
- Accessibility requirements
- A contact method
- Any timing constraints

Share essential needs early. A restaurant can only confirm what it can accommodate when it has accurate information.

## Review terms before paying

When checkout is added in a future milestone, the booking flow should present the final price, currency, cancellation policy, inclusions, and payment terms before money is taken. Check that the package name and guest count match your selection.

Avoid assuming availability based on a catalogue page. A future booking service will need to confirm the actual date and time. ExperienceHub does not currently publish unsupported availability information.

## Keep the confirmation

After a genuine booking, retain the confirmation and review arrival guidance. If something important changes, contact the booking provider using the details in the confirmation.

Until ExperienceHub’s checkout is implemented, its booking-information pages are simply the final step in exploring the demo journey. They are designed to explain what will happen later without creating a reservation.
